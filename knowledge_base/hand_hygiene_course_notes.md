# Course notes — Hand hygiene in clinical practice (Year 1)

*St Aldric School of Health Sciences (fictional institution). Teaching notes based on the WHO Guidelines on Hand Hygiene in Health Care (2009).*

## 1. Why hand hygiene matters

Healthcare workers' hands are the most common vehicle for transmitting microorganisms between patients. Hand hygiene is the single most effective measure for preventing healthcare-associated infections.

## 2. The WHO "My 5 Moments for Hand Hygiene"

Hand hygiene must be performed at five key moments: (1) before touching a patient, (2) before a clean or aseptic procedure, (3) after a risk of exposure to body fluids, (4) after touching a patient, and (5) after touching a patient's surroundings.

## 3. Alcohol-based hand rub

Alcohol-based hand rub is the preferred method for routine hand hygiene when hands are not visibly soiled. It is faster, more effective against most microorganisms and better tolerated by the skin than handwashing. The full procedure takes 20 to 30 seconds.

## 4. When to use soap and water instead

Hands must be washed with soap and water when they are visibly dirty or soiled with blood or other body fluids, after using the toilet, and when exposure to spore-forming organisms such as Clostridioides difficile is suspected or confirmed, because alcohol does not kill spores. Soap and water are also recommended during norovirus outbreaks. The full handwashing procedure takes 40 to 60 seconds.

## 5. Gloves

Wearing gloves does not replace hand hygiene. Hand hygiene must be performed before putting on gloves and immediately after removing them. Gloves must be changed between patients and between a contaminated and a clean body site on the same patient.

## 6. Skin care

Frequent hand hygiene can cause dry and irritated skin, which in turn harbours more bacteria. Students should use the hand cream provided on the wards and report any persistent dermatitis to Occupational Health.

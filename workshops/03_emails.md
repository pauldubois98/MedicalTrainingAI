# Workshop 3: Emails and administrative communication

**Duration:** 45 min · **Tool:** any AI assistant · **Data:** the fictional emails in data/emails

**Goal:** save time on emails while staying in control of everything that is sent.

<div class="warn">

**Before pasting an email into a public AI tool:** remove names, email addresses, student numbers and any health information. Replace them with [Student], [Colleague]… The examples provided are fictional.

</div>

## Exercise 1: Draft and adapt the tone (10 min)

Write a short draft (a few rough notes are enough), then ask:

```
Write an email from me (OSCE coordinator) announcing that the
OSCE timetable is now on the student portal. Key points:
- main session 18–22 January 2027; check your personal slot
- reasonable adjustment requests by 1 December 2026
- phones and smartwatches must be left in lockers
Write 3 versions: (a) to Year 3 students, friendly and clear;
(b) to the examiners, concise; (c) to the Dean, formal, 3 lines.
```

**Compare** the three versions. What changed? Would you send them as they are?

## Exercise 2: Summarise a thread and extract actions (10 min)

Paste the thread **01_thread_osce_organisation** and ask:

```
Summarise this thread in 5 bullet points. Then list all action
items in a table: who, what, deadline, status (done / open).
Finally, list the open questions and the risks for the exam.
```

**Check against the thread:** did it find the Friday SP shortage, the S207 renovation, the manikin battery issue and Sofia's unavailability on 20 January? Are all the deadlines correct (13 Nov, 4 Dec, 4 Jan, 8 Jan)?

## Exercise 3: A difficult email (10 min)

Read **02_student_complaint**. Ask for a reply, giving the AI the facts (anonymised):

```
Help me reply to an upset student who failed an OSCE station
because of a critical safety error (patient identity not checked).
Facts: [paste the facts list]. Tone: calm, respectful, firm on the
rule, empathetic. Acknowledge the emotion, explain the rule and
where it was published, explain the review procedure and the
feedback timeline, and say the comment about the examiner will be
looked into. Max 200 words. Do not promise a re-mark.
```

**Discuss:** is the tone right? Is anything promised that you cannot deliver? What would you change before sending?

## Exercise 4: Reusable replies for repetitive questions (15 min)

Paste the 6 questions in **03_repetitive_student_questions**, together with the relevant St Aldric documents (or use your NotebookLM notebook from Workshop 2):

```
Here are 6 student emails and our official documents.
For each email, write a short, friendly reply based only on the
documents, citing the section. If the documents do not answer the
question, say what is missing instead of inventing.
Then group the questions into themes and write one reusable
template per theme, with [placeholders].
```

**Check:** is every answer consistent with the documents? (e.g. adjustments by 1 December, 1 extra minute of reading time; medical certificate within 72 hours; no patient photos.)

## Going further: automation, with you in control

- **Templates** in your email client (Outlook *Quick Parts / My Templates*, Gmail *Templates*) for the replies you wrote today.
- **Assistants built into email** (Copilot in Outlook, Gemini in Gmail) can summarise threads and draft replies, if your institution provides them.
- **Rule of thumb:** the AI drafts, **you read, correct and send**. Never let an AI send emails on your behalf without checking them, especially to students or about grades.
